Tokens, base styles, and components for building on-brand CSA pages, apps, and prototypes. Every class below is prefixed csa- so it never collides with cloudsecurityalliance.org's own CSS.

Tokens

Color palette

CSA's primary palette, the three brand sub-palettes (Cloud, AI, Zero Trust), and the full extended 7×11 ramp. Hex values render live from tokens/colors.css; CMYK/PMS are print specs from the brand guide and can't be derived from a hex value, so those are entered directly.

CSA's primary colors define the core of the brand. Blue and orange work together as complementary colors — balancing trust, stability, and energy. Use consistently for logos, primary actions, and high-visibility moments.

The Cloud palette builds on CSA's core blues to create a sense of clarity, openness, and scale. A range of blue tones introduces depth and flexibility while maintaining a cohesive, digital feel rooted in the core brand. Use for cloud-related products, content, and environments.

The AI palette shifts the visual emphasis to warm tones to express innovation, intelligence, and forward momentum. Yellow Orange and Red Orange act as primary colors, creating a bold, high-energy palette supported by CSA Blue for balance and contrast. Use to highlight AI-driven concepts and experiences.

The Zero Trust palette centers on teal tones to communicate security, control, and reliability. These colors introduce a distinct visual identity while remaining grounded in CSA's core palette for consistency and trust. Use for Zero Trust initiatives and security-focused content.

7 families × 11-step ramps (25–800), named --{letter}{ramp}. Swatches below are generated from the actual CSS custom properties, not hardcoded.

Why now

Enterprise AI is entering a new era of failure — from two directions

Data loss, destructive automation, and failed recovery will rise sharply as AI agents spread, and there are no fool-proof preventions in sight. Resilience is what lets an organization take the hit and keep operating.

Direction 1: Adversarial

The agentic surface is under attack

Attackers increasingly target AI agents, model-driven workflows, non-human identities, and automated enterprise systems.

Direction 2: Systemic

Systems fail on their own

Misalignment, excessive permissions, hallucinated reasoning, and rushed deployments produce failures with no attacker at all.
